Agentic Trader — whole-machine blueprint

Complete system architecture including the regime allocation layer · 2026-07-24

Six layers, one direction of flow. The top layer decides how hard each desk may push; the middle layers decide what to trade and whether risk allows it; the bottom layers execute and remember. Nothing skips a layer, and every behavioural switch is an environment variable rather than a code change.

Layers: allocation (CIO) signal risk execution memory data

Layer 0 · Data

Two independent pipes, one scale contract

Finnhub (paid) serves every live strategy: daily bars, 1-minute bars, quotes — behind a rate limiter and per-endpoint circuit breakers. Layer 1 · Allocation — the CIO

regime_allocation.py — classifies the market, sizes the desks

Generates no signals and opens no positions. Once per day it answers: given the market state, how aggressive should each strategy be? Every input is measured from bars the scanners already cached — zero extra API calls:

  • Trend — EMA50 vs EMA200 separated ≥1%, price vs EMA200, a tolerant 20-day slope, and ADX ≥ 20. ADX gates strength in both directions: a structurally-ordered but momentum-less market is drift, and drift belongs in SIDEWAYS. Missing ADX never vetoes.
  • Volatility — SPY ATR% vs its own 100-day median (realized, not implied), and it must persist: a single shock day logs as SPIKE and does not re-allocate; only sustained elevation becomes VOLATILE.
  • Breadth — three participation measures: % of the 68 above their own EMA50, % above EMA200, and share of sectors leading (a majority of members above EMA50). The sector measure is what separates a broad advance from narrow leadership — a raw name-count can be carried by one crowded sector.

Persistence — a label becomes effective only after REGIME_PERSIST_DAYS (default 3) consecutive sessions agree on it; until then the last confirmed regime stays in force. Implemented statelessly: the previous sessions are re-classified from the same cached bars rather than tracked in a counter, because per-run state does not survive a redeploy — the same history always yields the same effective regime. A one-day shock is logged and ignored; a real change takes effect on its third session.

Confidence (0–100) scores how decisively each factor reads — distance from its own threshold, not a probability. Multipliers are then blended toward 1.00 in proportion to confidence: a fresh, weakly-evidenced regime barely moves sizing, and the lean grows as the evidence does. That gives gradual transitions without a calendar — chosen over day-by-day interpolation because it is stateless (nothing to reset on redeploy) and because transition speed should be driven by evidence rather than an arbitrary schedule.

STRONG_BULL leans hardest on cross-sectional momentum and pullback, the strategies that benefit most from broad persistent advances — an unvalidated preference, flagged as such. Cells are multipliers on sizing an engine already computed, not target capital percentages — normalised so the ordinary state (WEAK_BULL) is 1.00 everywhere. Consequence: switching this on in a normal market changes nothing; it only leans in or out as conditions leave the middle. Floor 0.25 — a regime may shrink a desk but never halt one (halting belongs to the risk-off gate and the kill switch: one mechanism per job).

Status: shadow. REGIME_ALLOC=shadow classifies and logs the multipliers while applying none of them. The percentages are a hypothesis — the proposal that suggested them said as much — so they earn their way to live on logged evidence, like every other switch in this system.

Layer 2 · Signal — five strategies, four live

Scanners and engines

One scanner pass per cycle produces funnel lines that name the gate which killed every candidate — the reason a "zero-signal" day is explainable rather than suspicious. Each engine then applies its own gates before the broker sees anything.

Layer 3 · Risk — four independent brakes

Each answers a different question; none substitutes for another

MechanismQuestion it answersAction
kill_switchHas today already lost too much? Is the feed dead?halt new entries; flatten intraday on price loss
regime.pyIs the market above its 200-day line?hold entries (exits never gated); fails open
portfolio_risk.pyHow much is at risk across all desks at once?logs heat; gates only when a ceiling is set. Counts a stopless position at full notional — unprotected is unbounded, not small
per-engine capsToo many positions in this one strategy?reject signal

Sizing chain, in order: 1% equity risk per trade → 10%-of-equity notional cap → regime multiplier → conviction tier. Multipliers scale share counts, never the equity passed to position_size — scaling equity would quietly loosen the notional cap as a side effect of a sizing preference.

Layer 4 · Execution

brokers.py → Alpaca — where the scar tissue lives

  • Bracket orders: entry + a GTC protective stop resting at the broker 24/7, so positions survive process death.
  • Idempotent client order IDs — a restart cannot duplicate an entry (the Jul-17 double-stack fix).
  • Fill polling: books record real fills, not quote estimates.
  • Boot reconcile: Alpaca is source of truth; true entry_time restored from the registry so holding-period logic survives redeploys; unknown holdings halt rather than trade blind; a failing order API now retries and reports "unverified", not "orphan".
  • Local stops act only while the market is open — firing on a stale after-hours quote is what caused the Jul-16 liquidations.
  • Known limits: no shorting, no partial sells. These block stat-arb, layered scale-outs, and partial profit-taking — the one genuine infrastructure project left.

Design rules the machine obeys

  • Every new behaviour ships dark. Shadow → live on logged evidence, never on a plausible argument. Six experiments have run this way.
  • Overlays fail open. A protective layer that cannot get data reports "no opinion" loudly rather than blocking trading — a safety feature must never become a new deadlock class.
  • Derive state, don't store it. Per-position flags reset on redeploy; anything derivable from persisted fields (initial risk, breakeven status, entry ATR, days held) is computed, not saved.
  • One mechanism per job. Regimes size, gates halt, caps count, the kill switch stops the day. Overlapping authority is how contradictory exits happen.
  • Reducing risk is never gated. Exits, stops, and flattens ignore every entry filter.
  • Flags are facts, not inferences. The boot banner states them; no analyst — human or AI — gets to assert a switch state from behaviour.

What the machine still owes itself

Every item here is evidence or infrastructure, not a new strategy:

  • The autopsy — autopsy.py against the audit trail: why swing bled.
  • Two swing backtest windows — old swing vs swing_v2 (the trend-pullback engine) vs the filtered-breakout variant vs hold-SPY. This decides whether pullback goes live, which is the question a second pullback engine would only duplicate.
  • The xsect replay — prices the sector cap and the five universe additions on history.
  • Partial-sell support in brokers.py — unblocks layered exits for meanrev and intraday, and is the prerequisite for any future short-side work.
  • Regime allocation → live — after its shadow log shows the classifier tracking real market states.

One sentence: the CIO decides how hard to push, five strategies decide what to buy, four brakes decide whether risk allows it, the broker layer executes with the stops resting outside the process, and /data remembers all of it — with every switch stated out loud at boot.
